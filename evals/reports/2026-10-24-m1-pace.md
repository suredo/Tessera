## How it is measured

## What slowed it, and what helped

- **Review found real defects, and fixing them took rounds.** Of the 25 pull requests whose reviews were counted (#43, #56, #57, #59 to #64, #66, #67, #70, #71, #73, #75, #77, #78, #82, #85 to #91; the smallest fixes and one-line docs changes were left out), Codex reviewed them in 50 rounds and reported at least 34 findings; only 7 passed on the first round. Most were in the security-sensitive bridge and storage: a check that could hang on a shared reference (#82), a forgeable "the person clicked" signal that needed a new design (#85), and a read that could overwrite a built-in function in the server (#88). Each finding was a defect worth catching. The cost is time: **#46, sized M, took 7 pull requests over two days**, about twice an M elsewhere.
- **Flaky tests.** Four turned up under load (#65, #79, #81, #83). The rule of quarantining at once with an issue (`ENG-20`) kept them from blocking work, but each cost a diagnosis. Two remain open (#81, #83) and must close before M1 does.
- **Local checks.** Running every check, browser tests included (about five minutes), on every change and every re-review added up. `ENG-31` (2026-10-24) now runs the checks a change can affect and leaves the full suite to CI, which should shorten re-reviews from here.
- **Helped:** the plan's split into small pull requests kept reviews focused and merges quick (median 3.2 hours); CI on a public repository costs no Actions minutes; decisions the owner made quickly (activation on first real input, what the server checks) kept the bridge moving.
